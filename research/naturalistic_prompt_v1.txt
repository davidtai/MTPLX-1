I've been thinking about how people form long-term habits, and I'd like your help reasoning through it rather than just getting a list of tips. Most advice on habit formation treats willpower as the scarce resource: pick a small enough action, chain it to an existing routine, and repeat until it becomes automatic. That framing is useful but incomplete, because it says nothing about why some habits survive a stressful week and others collapse the moment the schedule changes.

Here's what I want to explore with you. Consider three people who all decide to start exercising: one ties it to a fixed time of day no matter what, one ties it to a fixed trigger event like finishing dinner, and one just tries to "do it most days" without a specific anchor. My intuition is that the fixed-trigger person does best under disruption, because the trigger can survive a schedule change even when the clock time can't, while the vague intention collapses first because it has no reliable cue at all. Tell me whether that intuition actually holds, especially for people whose days are genuinely irregular, like shift workers or new parents.
